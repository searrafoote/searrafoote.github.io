About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Planetary Sciences at the University of Arizona's Lunar and Planetary Laboratory, working with Dr. Tyler D. Robinson. My research focuses on developing simulations to inform how future space telescopes, such as NASA’s Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO), will detect and characterize Earth-like planets. I work with the EXOSIMS yield modeling tool to integrate phase-dependent spectrally derived SNRs, with the goal of testing optimal parameters for HWO and evaluating SNR requirements for a detecting a range of biosignatures across planetary phase angles and eventually atmospheric compositions.

Research

Dissertation Work

Chapter 1: The Impact of Planetary Phase Functions on Exo-Earth Detectability with EXOSIMS

Most mission yield studies assume planets scatter light like an idealized (Lambertian) sphere. I replace that assumption with realistic, observationally motivated Earth phase functions and show how it changes which exo-Earths a Habitable Worlds Observatory–like mission can detect and characterize.
Foote et al. (2026), The Astrophysical Journal · Read the paper

Chapter 2: From Exo-Earth Detection to Atmospheric Characterization: Enabling Phase-Dependent Molecular Detection SNR in EXOSIMS

Building molecular detection directly into mission simulations: instead of one fixed signal-to-noise target, the required SNR for detecting O2 and H2O changes with the planet's orbital phase.
Foote et al. (2026), arXiv:2611.10342 · Read the paper

Chapter 3: Earth Through Time: Assessing Molecular Detectability and Characterization Yields for the Habitable Worlds Observatory with EXOSIMS

Extending the phase-dependent framework to CH4 and CO2 in Archean and Proterozoic Earth atmospheres, to predict how well the Habitable Worlds Observatory could characterize planets resembling earlier stages of Earth's history.
Foote et al., coming soon
